## The product, in one paragraph

PlainTicker Mobile is an Android app for the Solana Seeker, a crypto phone with a hardware wallet.
It reads tokenised US stocks (xStocks: TSLAx, NVDAx) on Solana and states facts about a token
before you swap into it: what it tracks, what the issuer can still do to it, how far the quoted
price has drifted from the NYSE close, and how deep the pool behind that quote is. It gives no
verdict and never says buy, sell, hold or avoid. Its one distinctive behaviour is a refusal: below
$10,000 of pool depth it will not print a tracking figure at all and states the pool instead, "Pool
holds $80, too thin to track". Measured 2026-09-12: of 160 analysed tokens Jupiter reports a depth
for only 53; UBERx quoted a 152.13 percent premium on a pool holding $80.

Design system, "Instrument": Canvas `#0B0F14`, Ink `#E8ECF1`, exactly one accent `#5AA9E6` meaning
interactive or live, radius 0 on everything, no gradients, no shadows, monospace for every number.
Outfit for words, JetBrains Mono for every number and ticker: "numbers never appear in Outfit."

## The marks

Image 1 shows all seven at the measured 60.7dp drawer size at 1:1, each with a 24dp copy beside it.

- **overhang**: a bar with a narrow footing 15 units off its centre; 44 of its 64 units hang over
  nothing. A printed figure standing on a pool too narrow to carry it.
- **overhang-flush**: the same with the footing at the near end.
- **floor**: a full-width rule with a column that stops 12 units short of it. The $10,000 liquidity
  threshold, and a reading that does not reach it. Everything above the rule is empty because
  nothing is printed up there.
- **kept-place**: three places on one row, two holding a figure, the third kept and left blank; the
  row is not shortened and the accent runs under the empty place.
- **short-measure**: the same two figures, and in the third place a stub a quarter of the height a
  figure needs. The stub is the reason the figure is not printed; the space above it is the figure
  that is not printed. The ratio is declared as a lie of scale: $80 against $10,000 is 1 to 125.
- **withheld-print**: an Accent field with one figure-shaped hole and nothing else.
- **gauge-inverted**: not a candidate. This is the mark on the phone now, inverted so that it has
  the edge the measurement says it never had. It is in the sheet so that a claim in the product
  marketing document, *"The brand mark is the tracking gauge itself rather than a letter, because
  it is the one mark nobody else has and it says what the app does"*, can be tested against a
  drawing instead of argued about.
