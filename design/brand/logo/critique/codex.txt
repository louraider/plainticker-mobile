1. **The split reads as two fonts in a logo.** At headline size, the seam divides a dense, rounded “Plain” from a wider, mechanically spaced “Ticker.” Matching cap heights does not match their texture. The justification also misapplies the product’s typography rule: “Ticker” is a word, not a ticker symbol. In running text, the switch becomes an interruption within the name. In a browser tab, where the title uses browser typography, the distinction disappears entirely; the name must work without it. I would ship the all-Outfit setting. It reads as one name, and the product can establish its numerical typography where actual numbers appear.

2. **Short-measure does not answer the earlier objection.** It shows a third bar with a smaller value. Nothing in the drawing establishes that the stub measures support while the tall bars represent readings supported by something else. They occupy the same baseline and use the same rectangular vocabulary, so the eye assigns them the same kind of quantity. Your explanation supplies the causal relationship; the mark supplies a bar chart. Worse, the product refuses to print an unreliable figure, while this mark visibly prints a small figure. The empty area above it is ordinary chart background, not an observable act of withholding. The exaggerated scale is secondary to that categorical mistake.

3. **Kept-place survives reduction better.** At the smaller size, its blue segment remains a clean ending to the baseline; short-measure adds a tiny step that reads as a low bar or a rendering irregularity. But kept-place does not visibly reserve a third place. There is no repeated container, divider or other structure making that empty region a slot. There are two uprights and an extended foot. The cleaner mark therefore loses the claimed explanation, but short-measure never secured that explanation either. This is a choice between a cleaner generic symbol and a more explicit chart—not between two successful drawings of refusal.

4. **The paper field provides a boundary and disguises the tile within the drawer’s prevailing treatment.** Those are different outcomes, and the contrast measurement establishes only the first. The paper version is plainly easier to locate than the Canvas version. But beside the other light tiles, its identity must come from the interior mark, and that is where this candidate remains weak. Preserving two colours preserves a small blue terminal; it does not make that terminal meaningful. Also, the paper treatment preserves the two-colour structure, not literally Ink and Accent: the main strokes become dark. Ship paper for visibility, but stop treating its contrast ratio as evidence of distinctiveness.

5. **Overhang** reads as an off-centre capital T or a top-alignment control. **Overhang-flush** reads as a thick upper-left corner or crop bracket. **Floor** reads as a top rule with a detached insertion cursor beneath it. **Kept-place** reads as a pause button resting on a tray; the blue ending looks like a status attachment. **Short-measure** reads as a bar-chart or equalizer control. **Withheld-print** reads as a selected text cursor in the large field and an outlined zero in the narrow lockup. Its changing outer proportions make that association unstable. Of these six, overhang is furthest from a familiar system control as drawn: its imbalance gives it a particular silhouette. That distance still does not communicate inadequate backing.

6. **The tracking-gauge sentence fails.** At the larger size, gauge-inverted looks like a chart-axis fragment with an intersecting cross. At the smaller size, it becomes a compact arrangement of ticks without a readable measurement. There is no reference, range or relationship that identifies tracking, much less tracking a token against its underlying stock. The blue field improves visibility without improving that explanation. “Nobody else has” is an unsupported exclusivity claim, and “says what the app does” assigns the drawing information it does not contain. Replace the sentence with: “The brand mark is an abstract symbol derived from the app’s tracking gauge.”

7. Set the **kept-place mark’s height in the horizontal lockup to exactly 1.00 times the wordmark’s cap height**, scaling it uniformly and retaining its current vertical centring. That is the drawing I would ship with the all-Outfit wordmark and paper launcher field. The current oversized symbol gives a generic pause-and-tray shape more authority than its meaning earns.
