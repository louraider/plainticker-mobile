Neither family should ship on its present reasoning. At 60.7dp, these are mostly readable drawings of generic interface controls. The product explanations supply meanings that the drawings do not contain.

The physical legibility is better than the conceptual legibility. In image 1, no pair of readings fuses, no pool disappears, and no bracket loses a corner. Even the small square survives in the half-resolution drawer rows. Calling those elements illegible would be false. The failure happens at the actual drawer size: the shapes resolve immediately into familiar punctuation and layout controls. There is no demonstrated smaller collapse size in these images, and inventing one would add nothing.

The 3.05 mask charges the bleeding bars at their ends. In `live-bar-bleed-ground` and `live-bar-bleed-inverted`, it cuts several device pixels into the outside tips, leaving sloped terminations. The narrower true-weight versions lose less. The readings remain untouched. In the inverted versions, the more consequential effect is the blue segment left of the black bar: it tapers into the mask and reads as a separate sidebar or binding. The bar has become a gutter dividing the tile. `live-bar-one-reading-inverted` inherits exactly that problem.

The contained bar in `live-bar-as-proposed` escapes those cuts. The quote drawings also keep their brackets intact, but their right-hand corners crowd the rounded shoulders. `quote-heavy-bracket-inverted` pays most: the blue margin pinches sharply around the bracket’s upper and lower outside corners. Its enlarged bracket consumes the space that was supposed to make the concept meaningful. The mask does not destroy these drawings; it exposes how heavily they depend on rectangular composition inside a rounded boundary.

`live-bar-as-proposed` is closest to a text-editing glyph: a caret beside two lines of text. Its secondary reading is a disassembled capital F. A person flicking past sees an editor or formatting control. The contained blue member looks substantial and structural, which also defeats the claimed reference to a delicate live rule.

`live-bar-bleed-ground` trades that caret for the divider in a sidebar-layout glyph. Dropping the readings changes their placement, not their identity as placeholder text. `live-bar-bleed-inverted` makes the sidebar reading stronger because the dark divider cuts an otherwise continuous blue tile into two regions. The hamburger escape worked only in the narrowest sense: it exchanged a menu-related resemblance for a pane-layout resemblance.

`live-bar-true-weight-ground` is the same sidebar-and-content glyph with a thinner divider. `live-bar-true-weight-inverted` is the same glyph reversed out of blue. The latter has the clearest visual hierarchy of the live-bar drawings. That does not make it specific. It would serve a log viewer, note-taking app or code editor without alteration, and a podcast player could claim the stripe as a progress position and the dashes as episode information just as convincingly as this app claims live readings.

The “true weight” defence confuses proportion with identity. Preserving the interface bar’s aspect ratio does not preserve its behaviour, context or apparent weight. This bar does not breathe. It does not sit beside a changing value. It runs through the entire launcher tile. The viewer sees a panel divider because that is what the drawing gives them.

`live-bar-one-reading-inverted` removes the only suggestion of multiple readings and leaves a minus sign beside a full-height divider. Its nearest general UI reading is a pane-collapse or remove control. At a glance, the minus wins. Simplification has made the icon easier to parse and given it a worse meaning: subtraction, minimisation or an empty pane.

Inversion costs this family its stated premise. On the Canvas versions, blue at least singles out the vertical member. On the inverted versions, the purported live bar becomes a dark absence while the surrounding field receives all the Accent. The feature said to anchor the identity is no longer the accented feature. Blue now functions as a tile colour. Calling the whole tile interactive cannot rescue that distinction; every launcher icon is interactive.

`quote-as-proposed` reads as a closing square bracket with a status dot beside it. The bracket alone is code punctuation, not a quotation mark and not a market quote. The complete arrangement also resembles an incomplete sign-out glyph: a doorway with its directional component missing. The blue circle gets read as a status indicator because coloured dots already have that familiar UI role. Nobody needs to reach tokenised-stock liquidity to explain what they see.

`quote-square-pool-ground` replaces the status indicator with a stop-button square beside a closing bracket. At a glance, it is still UI punctuation. `quote-square-pool-inverted` strengthens both shapes without changing that reading. The square and bracket now look like two components of one abstract control, not quantities whose relationship is suspect.

`quote-small-pool-inverted` is the most literal punctuation drawing: a small square period followed by a closing bracket. The square remains visible in the drawer composite, but it becomes a minor mark attached to the bracket’s reading. It contributes no recognisable pool meaning. `quote-heavy-bracket-inverted` is a stop square beside a heavier closing bracket; enlarging both makes the pair more emphatic while shrinking the alleged gulf. It spends space to make the unsupported metaphor louder.

`quote-round-pool-ground` returns to the status-dot-and-bracket glyph. Growing the circle makes the notification or recording-light association stronger. It also makes the radius-zero violation more conspicuous. Squaring it was a legitimate correction to the shape language; returning to the circle demonstrates that the concept still depends on a familiar status symbol it has assigned a private meaning.

For comparison, `as-shipped` reads as a slider or tuning control: a vertical thumb crossing a horizontal track. In monochrome, the extra downward stem makes that construction less coherent. It is another generic control icon, and neither new family has escaped that category.

“The dot is not decoration, it is the $80” fails. The drawing contains no mapping from money to area, no reference quantity, no threshold and no indication that the amount prevents a calculation. Eighty dollars and eight million dollars could receive the same dot. The explanation identifies an intended referent; it does not make that referent visible. Without the explanation, the dot functions as decoration or status.

Growing the square from 8 to 12 units improves its prominence and more than doubles its area. That rescues the component’s visibility and damages the story of insufficiency. Matching its width to the bracket stroke gives it the authority of a deliberate, adequately sized module. The 16-unit pool in the heavy version pushes further in the same direction. Conversely, keeping it small only produces smaller punctuation. Neither choice establishes “too little to support this quote.”

The gulf is empty space. It contains no reference against which to read 152%, no link between price and liquidity, and no withheld result. Worse, all the quote variants retain a complete, assertive bracket regardless of the pool’s size. The app’s distinctive act is refusing to print a tracking figure when the supporting pool is inadequate. These marks show two things sitting apart. They do not show that one cannot justify the other.

Family one has the same product problem from another direction. “Live data with readings” describes an enormous class of apps. It also centres the availability of readings when this product’s defining behaviour is sometimes withholding one. The marks can be explained through the design system, but that is not the same as expressing the product. An internal component name is not an identity.

Image 3 does not introduce an additional figure-ground flip in the inverted drawings. They already have dark marks in a lighter blue field in image 1; monochrome substitutes a light field for that blue. The inversion happened when the Canvas-ground drawings were reversed. Their silhouettes survive the monochrome treatment.

What does disappear is the colour-based argument. In the ground live-bar drawings, the formerly blue bar becomes the same colour as the readings, leaving an undifferentiated layout glyph. In the ground quote drawings, the pool loses its separation from the bracket and becomes another piece of punctuation. The inverted drawings have already surrendered that distinction in colour, so monochrome exposes how little was left to lose. Geometry survives across both families; the claimed meanings do not.

This round should be rejected as a test of the reasoning. Keep the refusal as the criterion for evaluating identity, and stop accepting stories that require the component names, the $80 example or a caption before the shapes become relevant. More adjustments to weight and polarity will refine these two generic glyphs without repairing that failure.

If I had to ship one tomorrow, I would ship `live-bar-true-weight-inverted`, whose single worst fault is that it identifies a generic sidebar more readily than PlainTicker.
