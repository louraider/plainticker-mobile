# Attempt five: two critics on fourteen drawings

The founder kept two of attempt four's seven, number 2 (the live bar anchor) and number 6 (the
disconnected quote), and asked for another round on those two with Antigravity and Codex on it.
Twelve drawings were made first, six per family, each named for the one thing it changes. Both
critics were then given the same drawings and asked opposite questions. Antigravity, which invented
both concepts, was asked to develop them and told not to restart. Codex, which had never seen the
project, was asked to argue against them, and got the rendered tiles, the monochrome layer and the
real drawer strips as images rather than as geometry. Each critic asked for exactly one concrete
change; both changes were then drawn, which is why there are fourteen drawings and not twelve.

**The two sections below are quoted verbatim.** Neither has been edited, tightened, reordered or
paraphrased, and neither voice is laundered into mine. My adjudication is the third section and
nothing above it is mine.

Briefs as sent: `BRIEF-AGY.md` and `BRIEF-CODEX.md`. Raw replies: `critique/antigravity.txt` and
`critique/codex.txt`.

---

## Google Antigravity, gemini-3.1-pro-high, effort high, plan mode, sandbox, 2026-09-13

Answering the six numbered questions in `BRIEF-AGY.md`. Run: 50.5 s, 23,711 tokens, one turn.

> 1. For family one, I would ship live-bar-true-weight-inverted. I would move the bar two units to
> the right, to x=36. This widens the field on the left side to 18 units, making it a deliberate
> margin instead of an accidental sliver. For family two, I would ship quote-square-pool-inverted. I

> 2. The hamburger misread is not solved. Bleeding the bar off the canvas breaks the menu shape, but

>

> reads as a speck. Squaring the pool helps because it matches the bracket stroke width and removes

> original concept placed a round form against a rigid bracket. The square makes both shapes

> 4. I accept the contrast readings. The inverted variants are the only drawings that define the tile

> light measure, but knocking it out of a solid field makes it a heavy block.
>
> 5. I would ship family two, the disconnected quote. I rank it first because a solid field with a
> distinct knockout creates a single recognizable mass that stops a person's eye. Family one fails

> object at a glance.
>
> 6. Drawing six, live-bar-one-reading-inverted, misreads the original paragraph. The concept depends
> on multiple horizontal marks anchoring against the vertical bar to represent a list. Reducing the

## Codex, codex-cli 0.154.0, non-interactive, read-only sandbox, with the renders attached

Asked to argue against the same drawings, with three images: every tile at 60.7dp at 1:1, nine of
them composited into the real drawer beside Photos and the Play Store, and the monochrome layer.

> Neither family should ship on its present reasoning. At 60.7dp, these are mostly readable drawings
> of generic interface controls. The product explanations supply meanings that the drawings do not
> contain.
>
> The physical legibility is better than the conceptual legibility. In image 1, no pair of readings
> fuses, no pool disappears, and no bracket loses a corner. Even the small square survives in the
> half-resolution drawer rows. Calling those elements illegible would be false. The failure happens
> at the actual drawer size: the shapes resolve immediately into familiar punctuation and layout
> controls. There is no demonstrated smaller collapse size in these images, and inventing one would
> add nothing.
>
> The 3.05 mask charges the bleeding bars at their ends. In `live-bar-bleed-ground` and
> `live-bar-bleed-inverted`, it cuts several device pixels into the outside tips, leaving sloped
> terminations. The narrower true-weight versions lose less. The readings remain untouched. In the
> inverted versions, the more consequential effect is the blue segment left of the black bar: it
> tapers into the mask and reads as a separate sidebar or binding. The bar has become a gutter
> dividing the tile. `live-bar-one-reading-inverted` inherits exactly that problem.
>
> The contained bar in `live-bar-as-proposed` escapes those cuts. The quote drawings also keep their
> brackets intact, but their right-hand corners crowd the rounded shoulders. `quote-heavy-bracket-inverted`
> pays most: the blue margin pinches sharply around the bracket's upper and lower outside corners.
> Its enlarged bracket consumes the space that was supposed to make the concept meaningful. The mask
> does not destroy these drawings; it exposes how heavily they depend on rectangular composition
> inside a rounded boundary.
>
> `live-bar-as-proposed` is closest to a text-editing glyph: a caret beside two lines of text. Its
> secondary reading is a disassembled capital F. A person flicking past sees an editor or formatting
> control. The contained blue member looks substantial and structural, which also defeats the claimed
> reference to a delicate live rule.
>
> `live-bar-bleed-ground` trades that caret for the divider in a sidebar-layout glyph. Dropping the
> readings changes their placement, not their identity as placeholder text. `live-bar-bleed-inverted`
> makes the sidebar reading stronger because the dark divider cuts an otherwise continuous blue tile
> into two regions. The hamburger escape worked only in the narrowest sense: it exchanged a
> menu-related resemblance for a pane-layout resemblance.
>
> `live-bar-true-weight-ground` is the same sidebar-and-content glyph with a thinner divider.
> `live-bar-true-weight-inverted` is the same glyph reversed out of blue. The latter has the clearest
> visual hierarchy of the live-bar drawings. That does not make it specific. It would serve a log
> viewer, note-taking app or code editor without alteration, and a podcast player could claim the
> stripe as a progress position and the dashes as episode information just as convincingly as this
> app claims live readings.
>
> The "true weight" defence confuses proportion with identity. Preserving the interface bar's aspect
> ratio does not preserve its behaviour, context or apparent weight. This bar does not breathe. It
> does not sit beside a changing value. It runs through the entire launcher tile. The viewer sees a
> panel divider because that is what the drawing gives them.
>
> `live-bar-one-reading-inverted` removes the only suggestion of multiple readings and leaves a minus
> sign beside a full-height divider. Its nearest general UI reading is a pane-collapse or remove
> control. At a glance, the minus wins. Simplification has made the icon easier to parse and given it
> a worse meaning: subtraction, minimisation or an empty pane.
>
> Inversion costs this family its stated premise. On the Canvas versions, blue at least singles out
> the vertical member. On the inverted versions, the purported live bar becomes a dark absence while
> the surrounding field receives all the Accent. The feature said to anchor the identity is no longer
> the accented feature. Blue now functions as a tile colour. Calling the whole tile interactive
> cannot rescue that distinction; every launcher icon is interactive.
>
> `quote-as-proposed` reads as a closing square bracket with a status dot beside it. The bracket
> alone is code punctuation, not a quotation mark and not a market quote. The complete arrangement
> also resembles an incomplete sign-out glyph: a doorway with its directional component missing. The
> blue circle gets read as a status indicator because coloured dots already have that familiar UI
> role. Nobody needs to reach tokenised-stock liquidity to explain what they see.
>
> `quote-square-pool-ground` replaces the status indicator with a stop-button square beside a closing
> bracket. At a glance, it is still UI punctuation. `quote-square-pool-inverted` strengthens both
> shapes without changing that reading. The square and bracket now look like two components of one
> abstract control, not quantities whose relationship is suspect.
>
> `quote-small-pool-inverted` is the most literal punctuation drawing: a small square period followed
> by a closing bracket. The square remains visible in the drawer composite, but it becomes a minor
> mark attached to the bracket's reading. It contributes no recognisable pool meaning.
> `quote-heavy-bracket-inverted` is a stop square beside a heavier closing bracket; enlarging both
> makes the pair more emphatic while shrinking the alleged gulf. It spends space to make the
> unsupported metaphor louder.
>
> `quote-round-pool-ground` returns to the status-dot-and-bracket glyph. Growing the circle makes the
> notification or recording-light association stronger. It also makes the radius-zero violation more
> conspicuous. Squaring it was a legitimate correction to the shape language; returning to the circle
> demonstrates that the concept still depends on a familiar status symbol it has assigned a private
> meaning.
>
> For comparison, `as-shipped` reads as a slider or tuning control: a vertical thumb crossing a
> horizontal track. In monochrome, the extra downward stem makes that construction less coherent. It
> is another generic control icon, and neither new family has escaped that category.
>
> "The dot is not decoration, it is the $80" fails. The drawing contains no mapping from money to
> area, no reference quantity, no threshold and no indication that the amount prevents a calculation.
> Eighty dollars and eight million dollars could receive the same dot. The explanation identifies an
> intended referent; it does not make that referent visible. Without the explanation, the dot
> functions as decoration or status.
>
> Growing the square from 8 to 12 units improves its prominence and more than doubles its area. That
> rescues the component's visibility and damages the story of insufficiency. Matching its width to
> the bracket stroke gives it the authority of a deliberate, adequately sized module. The 16-unit
> pool in the heavy version pushes further in the same direction. Conversely, keeping it small only
> produces smaller punctuation. Neither choice establishes "too little to support this quote."
>
> The gulf is empty space. It contains no reference against which to read 152%, no link between price
> and liquidity, and no withheld result. Worse, all the quote variants retain a complete, assertive
> bracket regardless of the pool's size. The app's distinctive act is refusing to print a tracking
> figure when the supporting pool is inadequate. These marks show two things sitting apart. They do
> not show that one cannot justify the other.
>
> Family one has the same product problem from another direction. "Live data with readings" describes
> an enormous class of apps. It also centres the availability of readings when this product's
> defining behaviour is sometimes withholding one. The marks can be explained through the design
> system, but that is not the same as expressing the product. An internal component name is not an
> identity.
>
> Image 3 does not introduce an additional figure-ground flip in the inverted drawings. They already
> have dark marks in a lighter blue field in image 1; monochrome substitutes a light field for that
> blue. The inversion happened when the Canvas-ground drawings were reversed. Their silhouettes
> survive the monochrome treatment.
>
> What does disappear is the colour-based argument. In the ground live-bar drawings, the formerly
> blue bar becomes the same colour as the readings, leaving an undifferentiated layout glyph. In the
> ground quote drawings, the pool loses its separation from the bracket and becomes another piece of
> punctuation. The inverted drawings have already surrendered that distinction in colour, so
> monochrome exposes how little was left to lose. Geometry survives across both families; the claimed
> meanings do not.
>
> This round should be rejected as a test of the reasoning. Keep the refusal as the criterion for
> evaluating identity, and stop accepting stories that require the component names, the $80 example
> or a caption before the shapes become relevant. More adjustments to weight and polarity will refine
> these two generic glyphs without repairing that failure.
>
> If I had to ship one tomorrow, I would ship `live-bar-true-weight-inverted`, whose single worst
> fault is that it identifies a generic sidebar more readily than PlainTicker.

## Adjudication

Mine. Everything above is theirs.

### Where they agree, which is more than where they do not

**Both read the inverted live bar the same way, independently, and neither was told what the other
said.** Antigravity: "a void slicing vertically through the tile reads as a physical cut that
separates the field into two distinct pieces." Codex: "the blue segment left of the black bar ...
reads as a separate sidebar or binding. The bar has become a gutter dividing the tile." That is the
same finding from two temperaments, one of them looking at geometry and the other at pixels. It is
the finding of the round.

**Both dropped the defence of the dot.** Antigravity: at that scale a small isolated unit reads as
a speck. Codex, harder: eighty dollars and eight million dollars would get the same dot, so the
sentence names a referent without making it visible. The defence is withdrawn and should not be
restated in attempt six. What survives is narrower and still worth something: a square unit the
width of the bracket's own stroke reads as a deliberate module of the thing across the tile, and
both critics accept squaring it as a correction to the shape language even while rejecting what it
was supposed to mean.

**Both accept the measurement and neither argued the ground.** The numbers are in
`edge-contrast.json` and on every card.

### Where they disagree, and which I followed

**Which family survives.** Antigravity would ship the disconnected quote and drop the live bar
outright, on the grounds that the vertical void cuts the tile into columns that never register as
one object. Codex, forced to name one, named `live-bar-true-weight-inverted`, while saying in the
same sentence that its worst fault is identifying a generic sidebar more readily than this app, and
while arguing that neither family should ship at all.

I followed Antigravity. Codex's pick is a pick under protest, chosen for having the clearest visual
hierarchy of the live-bar drawings, and its own paragraph says that clearest does not mean specific
and lists a log viewer, a note-taking app, a code editor and a podcast player that would take the
same tile. Antigravity's reason is about the object and not about the hierarchy, and the object is
what a drawer tile is judged as. The two critics also agree, against Codex's own pick, that the
live bar's inverted form manufactures a pane divider. A pick that both critics' reasoning argues
against is not a pick.

**The two concrete changes Antigravity asked for.** Both are drawn, neither is adopted, and the
drawings are why.

`live-bar-wide-gutter-inverted` is Antigravity's instruction carried out exactly: the bar two units
right, so the Accent band beside it is 18 units instead of 14, "making it a deliberate margin
instead of an accidental sliver." In the drawer strip the wider band does not read as a margin. It
reads as more of a second pane, because the band was never ambiguous about being deliberate, it was
ambiguous about being a sidebar, and widening a sidebar makes a sidebar. That is Codex's objection
confirmed on sight, so I followed Codex.

`quote-tight-gulf-inverted` is the other instruction carried out exactly: the pool six units right,
gulf from 20 units to 14, "tightening the connection between the two shapes." Tightening the
connection is the thing that must not happen. The emptiness between the two is the only part of the
mark that is about this product, and at 14 units the pool and the bracket begin to read as two
components of one control, which is the failure Codex named at 20. I followed Codex.

Both critics get their ask drawn and both asks lose to the drawing. That is the only way this round
could settle anything, because both asks were plausible in prose.

### What I would ship

**`quote-square-pool-inverted`.** Edge 7.27 to 1 against the drawer's 1.03. The pool at 12 units is
10.1dp and 30 device pixels on the real tile, so nothing in it is near a floor. It breaks nothing
in DESIGN.md: squaring the pool restores section 4's radius 0 and takes the mark out of section 8's
coloured-dot clause, and section 2's one accent is kept by inverting rather than by adding. Its
silhouette is the least like anything else in that drawer. Against the 8-unit variant it is not
close; the small pool reads as a nick in the field rather than as a counterpart.

**From family one, `live-bar-true-weight-inverted` is the best of the six and I would not ship it.**
It is the answer to the scale complaint, 8 units across the 72 a launcher shows being the 1 to 9
the bar has beside its own label in the app, and it is the strongest tile in that family in the
drawer. It is also the drawing both critics say is a sidebar.

### The answer to problem two, which was to solve the misread and the scale or show why not

They cannot both be solved, and the reason is structural rather than a failure of drawing.
Escaping the hamburger needs the bar to stop being an inset object, which means bleeding it past
the mask. Getting an edge needs the field inverted, which the measurement settles. Do both and the
bleeding bar becomes a full-height cut through a solid field, which is a pane divider. Do neither
and it is a hamburger. Do only the first and the tile scores 1.39 to 1 and is not a tile. The scale
complaint on its own is answerable, and `live-bar-true-weight-*` answers it as far as arithmetic
allows: 2dp of a 48dp tile is 4.5 units, under the 6-unit floor, so the absolute width is
unreachable at any size and the proportion is what is left. Codex rejects even that, and its
sentence is worth keeping: preserving the aspect ratio does not preserve the behaviour, and this
bar does not breathe.

### The objection this round does not answer

Codex's deepest one, and it is left standing rather than argued with: neither family draws the
refusal, which is the one thing this app does that its neighbours do not. "These marks show two
things sitting apart. They do not show that one cannot justify the other." The mark that did draw
the refusal was attempt four's number 1, the withheld number, ranked first by Antigravity and not
kept by the founder. Nothing in attempt five recovers it, and no amount of weight or polarity on
these two families will.
